# Swap rules (draft)

> **Draft. Nothing here is decided.** This page is a starting point for discussion. See [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) and [how to have your say](have-your-say.md).

## The short version (for the sign)

Take parts. Leave parts. IoT and electronics only.

## Welcome

- Working dev boards and microcontrollers
- Sensors, displays, and modules
- Wire, jumpers, connectors, headers, and cables
- Resistors, capacitors, LEDs, and other small components
- Breadboards, enclosures, and mounting hardware

## Not welcome

- Loose lithium batteries of any kind, or devices with swollen or damaged batteries
- Anything that plugs into a wall outlet
- Broken or untested gear
- Soldering irons, blades, and other sharp or hot tools
- Chemicals, flux, and solder
- Anything that is not electronics

## How to leave a part

1. Test it if you can.
2. Put boards and chips in an anti-static bag. Put small parts in a zip bag.
3. Label the bag: what it is, and "tested working" or "untested".
4. Boards, sensors, and chips go in the sealed bin. Close the lid firmly. Everything else goes in the open bins.

## Who may throw things away

- **One named steward per box**, with the explicit authority to discard. The usual failure in comparable projects is not too much junk, it is a steward expected to keep the box nice but not allowed to remove anything.
- The steward's name, or the name of the person on shift, goes **on the sign** next to the rules. Authority nobody can see is authority nobody trusts.
- Anyone may take. Only the steward, or the person on shift, may discard, and discarding never needs an explanation or a photo.

## Keeping junk small

- **One category, stated on the box.** Boxes drift into "anything electrical" and then into "anything". A single stated category is the cheapest filter there is.
- **A size gate, not just a lid.** If a part does not fit through the opening, it cannot be donated. This removes most bulk dumping, a whole drawer, a mattress, a printer, without anyone reading a rule.
- **Date everything that goes in.** Anything older than the agreed window leaves without discussion. Without a date, "it might be useful" wins forever.
- **Emptyable in ten minutes.** If clearing the box takes an afternoon, it stops being cleared.
- **Waterproof, lidded, slightly raised.** Most of what gets called junk is weather damage, and a box that takes on water manufactures its own rubbish.
- **Closed at night if dumping appears.** It costs nothing and cuts anonymous overnight dumping.

## Handling what should not be there

- **No blame, no naming.** Shaming moves dumping to the kerb beside the box, which is worse than inside it.
- **A designated out bin**, emptied on the same trip. If the exit path is not obvious, the junk stays.
- **48 hours for mystery items.** Unidentified, unlabelled, or untestable parts get two days, then leave. "I will work out what it is later" is how a box dies.
- **Publish numbers, not people.** Items in, items out, junk removed, steward minutes.

## What to measure (per box, per week)

Items in, items out, junk removed (count or rough volume), steward minutes, and one photo.

- Junk volume over the threshold means **narrow the category**, not add more rules.
- Steward minutes over the threshold means **the box is in the wrong place**, not badly run.

## Good to know

- Parts are free and come with no guarantee. Use them at your own risk.
- Small parts are a choking hazard. Young children need an adult with them.
- Take what you will use. Leave some for the next person.
- If the box is damaged, wet inside, or out of bags, scan the QR code and open an issue.
