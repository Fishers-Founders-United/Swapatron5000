## 1. Introduction
Batteries are energy‑dense devices that can pose safety hazards if mishandled. This summary provides concise, actionable guidance for anyone working with batteries in the Swapatron5000 ecosystem.

---

## 2. General Safety Principles
- **Never** short‑circuit a battery terminal.
- **Avoid** exposing batteries to extreme temperatures, moisture, or direct sunlight.
- **Use** appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE): safety glasses, insulated gloves, and flame‑resistant clothing when handling large or high‑energy cells.
- **Inspect** batteries before use for swelling, leakage, corrosion, or physical damage.
- **Follow** manufacturer‑specified limits for charge/discharge rates.

---

## 3. Handling & Inspection
| Action | Recommended Practice |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Physical Inspection** | Look for bulging, dents, cracked casings, or electrolyte leakage. Discard any compromised cells.
| **Terminal Contact** | Use insulated tools; keep fingers away from terminals.
| **Polarity** | Verify correct polarity before connecting to circuits or chargers.
| **Static Discharge** | Ground yourself when handling sensitive lithium‑ion cells to prevent static‑induced sparks.

---

## 4. Storage Guidelines
- Store batteries in a **cool, dry** environment (15‑25 °C, < 60 % RH).
- Maintain a **state‑of‑charge (SOC)** of 40‑60 % for long‑term storage of lithium‑ion cells.
- Use **fire‑resistant containers** (e.g., Li‑ion safety bags) for bulk storage.
- Keep batteries **away from flammable materials** and sources of ignition.
- Label storage areas with **hazard warnings** and **capacity limits**.

---

## 5. Charging Best Practices
- Use only **approved chargers** that match the battery chemistry and voltage rating.
- **Never** leave charging batteries unattended for extended periods.
- Implement **charge termination** (voltage cut‑off) and **temperature monitoring**.
- For high‑capacity packs, employ **balancing circuits** to equalize cell voltages.
- Avoid charging **damaged** or **over‑discharged** cells.

---

## 6. Transportation & Packaging
- Follow **UN 38.3** and **IATA** regulations for shipping lithium batteries.
- Use **UN‑rated packaging** with proper cushioning and venting.
- **Label** packages with battery type, quantity, and hazard class.
- For **air transport**, keep batteries **protected against short circuits** (e.g., tape over terminals).

---

## 7. Disposal & Recycling
- **Do not** dispose of batteries in regular trash.
- Collect spent batteries in **designated recycling bins**.
- Follow local regulations for hazardous waste handling.
- For lithium‑ion cells, use **approved recycling facilities** that can recover valuable metals.

---

## 8. Emergency Response
| Situation | Immediate Action |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Fire** | Use **Class D** (metal) fire extinguisher or **CO₂** for lithium‑ion fires. Do **not** use water.
| **Leakage** | Evacuate area, wear PPE, contain spill with absorbent material, and notify hazardous‑materials team.
| **Injury** | Flush skin/eyes with water for at least 15 minutes; seek medical attention.
